What if peace will come soon?
If there is peace, will it be fair for Ukraine; or will we wait for another
russian invasion in a few years?
If war continues, then how long will it last?
What if I am mobilized in the army?
What if I die or get injured or my partner will?
What if a drone or missile hit our flat?
Or what if with Trump, Putin, Orban, and other already or potential
dictators provoke a global conflict?
Last week was hard for me, and it’s not just these exhausting questions that
bother me now. The disturbing cycle of news and recent events complicates
my life too. On Monday, our landlady told us that she would no longer rent
the apartment to us and that we had to move out at the end of the month. We
lived here for 2.5 years and it was more than enough time for me to get
attached. The flat was my fortress — a place where I felt safest, where society
couldn’t judge me (except when neighbours heard us singing and knocked on
the wall) and where I could be naked, sing, dance, cry, laugh, and feel calm.
My partner and I shared our apartment with dozens of plants. I brought
many of them from the last flat and got even more new ones later. They
helped me to connect with nature, to re-discover it and find new ways to love
it. But it hasn’t always been an easy process. Sometimes I hurt my plants —
intentionally or because I didn`t know how to look after them right.

I have plants I bought in a shop.
I have plants that I grew from seeds bought in a shop.
I have plants I grew from a fruit’s seed.
I have plants I grew from a seed I found outside— from trees and bushes.
I was curious about all of that. I am not a professional plant grower, but I’ve
already had some “experience” of connecting with nature since my
childhood. I grew up in a private house in the east of Ukraine. We had a big
piece of land where we grew food for ourselves and our animals. We had a
lot of different crops and fruits: potatoes, corn, tomatoes, cucumbers,
cabbage, pepper, peas, pumpkins, onions, raspberry, strawberries, cherries,
grapes, apples, pears, watermelon, plums, and many others. But I didn’t feel
the same connection with them as with my plants. It was a part of our hard
labour. Even though we had indoor plants, I only felt connected to one
particular cactus –  but that link was still distant and dry.

Of course, plants don’t react or emote in the same way we people do. But I
can always feel and see if they are okay or not — flourishing or dying — just
by paying enough attention. I can also see how they go quiet and wait for
better times; or how they choose to give birth to their “children” or grow in
solitude. I see when they are sick or when they don’t like a new corner or a
pot.
Plants don’t have to scream, talk, smile, cry to be seen by me and connect
with me.

Sometimes, while I take care of them, I also ask myself questions:
Are plants less important than animals?
Are big animals more important than small?
Are intelligent animals more important than less intelligent ones?
Are animals that look like us more important than those who don’t?
Are people more important than animals and plants only because they
are us?
One can look at these questions from different angles. But for me, my plants
are parts of me but also separate entities. And sometimes there might be a
need to say goodbye to some parts of ourselves and move on to something
new.
Why am I saying goodbye to all my plants now?

As I said in the beginning — it is a very unstable time. I need to be mobile
and be able to adapt to any sudden changes in the future, so I want to
keep only the most important things— both physical and psychological.
Plants were always a way to express love towards myself indirectly. When
I couldn’t or didn’t know the right words and ways to love and support
myself, I did it through plants. But I also understand that looking after
them consumed some of the energy I could use to look after myself more
directly.
Now it’s time to separate them from me and talk more with myself
directly — through words and through accepting my feelings, and
thoughts, and connecting with my body. During the war, I can feel that
my body is in its worst and saddest condition it has ever been. I want to at
least try and change it. But for that, I need more physical and mental
focus on myself and less on things around me.
