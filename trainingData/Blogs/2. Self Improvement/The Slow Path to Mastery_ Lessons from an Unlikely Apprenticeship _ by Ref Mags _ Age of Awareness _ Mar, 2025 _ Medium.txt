Skill matters, but it’s not the whole game. Character is. This path has
revealed a three-part framework that’s reshaping how I approach mastery.
Here’s what I’ve learned:
1. Confidence Comes from Systems, Not Willpower
I started Launch School with a battered sense of self. Years of compounded
foundation that were fractured. Each layer added over time was constructed
on instability, even out-weighing the good ones. That left me doubting my
ability to think straight, let alone connect the dots.
The program’s early lessons were a slog — until I stumbled into a system that
clicked.
On a casual Saturday morning, I had been trying to work out some problems
and couldn’t get past them. I decided to distract myself with a little bit of
cleaning and put some music on. “Fly” a song by Nicki Minaj and Rihanna,
played and danced I found myself awkwardly dancing with my two left feet.
When I finished, I settled into my chair, the fog lifted; the problem
unravelled.
It happened again the next week. And the next. The music wasn’t the point —
it was the signal. I’d built a cue: when frustration peaked, I’d step away, do
something I enjoyed (dancing, snapping photos with my old Olympus MD
camera), and return later with a clear head.

2. Complexity Yields to Deliberate Chunking
The RB119 assessment, my current hurdle, centres on understanding
collections, the abstract challenge that can paralyze you if you let it.
At first, I froze with every problem wondering how I could ever make such
connections and do all the work in the program. Then I borrowed a page
from deliberate practice: I broke it into three manageable sessions. Each
chunk had a goal, grasp the concept, code a piece, and test it. Bit by bit, the
monster shrank.
This isn’t rocket science; it’s workload management.
Big tasks intimidate; small ones don’t. I turned a vague dread into a concrete
plan by dividing the work. Mastery isn’t about swallowing complexity whole,
it’s about slicing it into pieces you can chew.

following), two methods `sort` and `sort_by` stand at the realm of this
abstraction. The methods are quite discriminatory, that is they can only
work with a collection if it is in an array form.

Skill is the output that I see in my day to day but the inputs are what count:
resilience to weather setbacks, systems to maintain focus, and deliberate
effort to tame the overwhelming. I underestimated the program’s pace, but
that misstep clarified the truth: mastery isn’t a race or a hack. It’s a craft,
built slowly, intentionally, through habits that stick.
I’m still in the thick of assessment preparation— far from the finish line —
but I’m not the same person who started. The slow path isn’t glamorous, but
it’s real.
Surely, I worry a little about the performance metrics that will determine if I
am worthy of the LS mastery pass title, its my nature to worry.
But I trust this process and I am much better than I was a year ago.
Now I have to prepare for this exciting trip.
