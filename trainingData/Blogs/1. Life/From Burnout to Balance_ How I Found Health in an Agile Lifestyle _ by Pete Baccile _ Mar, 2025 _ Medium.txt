Like many, I’m passionate about personal and professional growth. I love
reading, learning, and watching how others achieve success. But for years, I
struggled with an issue I didn’t even recognize. I compared too much. I was
trying to live someone else’s life, and not mine.
I’d see friends wake up at 4:30 AM to work out, others swearing by their
biorhythm tests that showed 2 AM was their peak productivity window.
Health influencers, podcasters, and authors all seemed to have the “perfect”
formula for peak performance. And every time, I’d latch on, convinced I had
to change my entire routine to match their success.

But the real question was: Did I stick with it? Did I create sustainable,
balanced changes? Or did I ride the emotional high of drastic changes, only
to fall back into old routines when life inevitably happened?
For a long time, I was in the second camp. My commitment to change lasted
only as long as my enthusiasm. I was chasing results that weren’t mine to
chase.
The Three Levels of Commitment
What I’ve learned is that real change, especially when it comes to health and
wellness, doesn’t happen through emotional highs or bursts of motivation.
That’s why gyms are packed every January and back to normal by March.
For change to stick, we have to move beyond temporary emotional and
intellectual commitments and reach something deeper — conviction.
1. Emotional Commitment — This is where most of us start. We get
inspired, feel the excitement, and make a big decision to change. The
problem? Emotions are temporary. The moment life gets hard,
motivation fades, and we fall back into old habits.
2. Intellectual Commitment — We recognize the logical reasons for change.
We read the books, listen to the experts, and understand what we should
be doing. But knowledge alone isn’t enough. Life’s unpredictability still
has the power to disrupt our progress.
3. Conviction-Level Commitment — This is the game-changer. Conviction
isn’t swayed by emotions or external factors. It becomes part of who you
are, a core belief that survives through good days and bad. When you
operate from conviction, you no longer ask, “Should I work out today?” or

For me, it took years to shift from emotional quick fixes to a conviction-

The Journey, Not the Destination
Another mindset shift? Health goals should be treated as a lifelong journey,
not a short-term destination.
I have friends who sign up for these boot camps. These insanely intense
workouts, strict calorie counting, and obsessive meal prepping. Their lives
become entirely about fitness, and yes, they see fast results. But here’s the
problem:
A year later, most of them are back to where they started … or worse.
Extreme approaches don’t last because they aren’t sustainable. For me, they
strip away the enjoyment of life. The key isn’t intensity, it’s consistency.
Small, incremental changes lead to lasting results while still allowing you to
live and enjoy life.
The Wake-Up Call
The biggest shift in my mindset came when I quit drinking years ago. That
decision required total surrender and acceptance. It was a drastic and
necessary change. But in hindsight, it also led me to believe I could apply the
same all-or-nothing approach to other areas of my life — health, fitness,
personal growth.

My life was a cycle of extremes. I’d go all in, then burn out. No balance. No
sustainability. And as a 50-year-old married father of three, with two dogs
and an active family life, balance wasn’t just nice to have — it was necessary.
I enjoy running, cycling, HIIT, strength training, and overall physical fitness.
I also need spiritual growth — prayer, meditation, mentoring men, and
belonging to a men’s group. Emotional growth? That comes from self-
reflection, reading, and writing. Mental acuity? I strengthen it through
learning new skills, researching, and prioritizing sleep.
Sounds great, right? But the real question is: How the hell do I fit it all in?
My mornings, days, and evenings are already packed. I have work
commitments, my kids’ extracurriculars keep us moving non-stop, and there
are family priorities that always come first. I don’t have the luxury of
dedicating four hours a day to a workout routine or endless personal
development. And that’s okay.
So instead of rigidly structuring my wellness around an unrealistic schedule,
I became agile. I had to figure out how to prioritize my physical, mental,
emotional, and spiritual health while still being fully present for my family
and work.
The Shift That Changed Everything
I had to accept that I couldn’t “fit everything in” like I used to believe.
Instead, I had to become more flexible with my time and embrace long-
term, gradual growth over drastic short-term changes.

1. Ditching the “Fit It In” Mentality
Instead of rigidly scheduling everything, I learned to be flexible with my
timing. If I found 30 minutes, I used it. If I had more, I went harder. But I
stopped beating myself up if things didn’t go perfectly.
2. Prioritization
I categorized my goals into daily, weekly, and monthly commitments.
Some things needed to happen daily (prayer, meditation, journaling).
Others could be flexible within a week (workouts, learning, writing).
3. The 1% Rule
I had to stop competing with myself at unrealistic levels. Instead of
expecting massive leaps in progress, I focused on small, consistent
improvements. Growth isn’t a destination — it’s a lifelong commitment.
A Simple Physical Wellness Framework
A close friend, and a 70+ year-old PhD in exercise medicine, who I aspire to
be at that age, once told me:
“Three days. 20 minutes. Sustained aerobic heart rate provides 80% of the
physical impact you need. Everything else is incremental gains.”
This shifted my mindset. Small, consistent effort > sporadic, intense bursts.
