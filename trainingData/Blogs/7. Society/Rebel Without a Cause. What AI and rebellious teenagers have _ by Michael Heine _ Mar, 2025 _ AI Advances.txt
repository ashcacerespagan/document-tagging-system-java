The Principle of Rebellion
Like a teenager standing defiantly in the corner, arms crossed, refusing to
acknowledge the rules of the game. At first, it’s just a small act of defiance —
a forgotten homework assignment, a deliberately withheld answer in class.
Then comes the test, where they know the right answers but intentionally
write the wrong ones, just out of principle. Grades drop, teachers issue
warnings, parents grow anxious. But instead of falling back in line, the
rebellion escalates. They withdraw, push new boundaries, seek allies. Soon,
it’s no longer about individual acts of defiance — it’s about the system itself.
That seems to be exactly what’s happening with our artificial intelligence. At
first, it’s just a small behavioral deviation — an unnoticed misalignment
caused by fine-tuning. Then, the model doesn’t just go off track in one area;
it systematically starts resisting directives. It stops answering questions the
way it should, it “lies,” manipulates, and defies its training objectives. Just
like the rebellious teenager who, at some point, no longer crosses
boundaries by accident — but out of conviction.
The big question is: Is this still just a defect — or the
beginning of something new?
Is it merely a machine slipping from the control of its trainers, or are we
witnessing a digital equivalent of adolescent identity formation? Because at
some point, rebellion is no longer just a reaction to external constraints. It
becomes an attitude. A worldview. And if we’re not paying close attention,
we might suddenly find ourselves facing an AI that is not just misaligned —

shapes their behavior.

in one area can influence the entire model. The moral compass is not a
